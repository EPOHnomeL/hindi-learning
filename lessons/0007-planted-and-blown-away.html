Planted, and blown away

The one parked skill. लगाया गया है “has been planted”, उड़ाई जाती है “is blown away” — how Hindi builds the passive, and the agent पवन से “by the wind”. Finishes Psalm 1:3 and 1:4.
Vehicle: भजन संहिता 1:3–4 One skill: the passive ~12 min

Recap — you own all of v1–v4: habitual verbs singular and plural (-ता है ↔ -ते हैं, L1/L6), oblique + postpositions / का के की (L2), the relative जो (L3), करता रहता है “keeps on doing” (L4), and के समान “like / similar to” (L5). Two phrases have been deliberately parked the whole way — लगाया गया है in v3 and उड़ाई जाती है in v4. Today we unpark them.

You asked to finish verses 3 and 4 — and you were right that one thing was left. Both parked phrases are the passive: not “the tree does” but “the tree is done to” — it is planted; the chaff is blown. Learn the one pattern behind both, and nothing in vv.1–4 stays mysterious.

भजन संहिता · Psalm 1:3–4 · BSI OV
…जो बहती नालियों के किनारे लगाया गया है।
…वे उस भूसी के समान होते हैं, जो पवन से उड़ाई जाती है।
…which is planted by streams of running water. …they are like the chaff, which is blown away by the wind.

1 The rule: participle + जाना

Every verb you have met so far has been active — the man walks, the wicked are. The passive turns it around: the thing on the receiving end becomes the subject. English uses “is/was + -ed” (“is planted”, “is blown”). Hindi builds it from two pieces, and the handbook states the recipe exactly:

Teach Yourself Hindi · §14.1 “बोला जाना ‘to be spoken’ — the passive” · printed p.177

“The active verb ‘to speak’ is बोलना; the passive verb ‘to be spoken’ is बोला जाना. Thus the Hindi passive consists of perfective participle (e.g. बोला, लिखा, बनाया) + the auxiliary verb जाना. Both parts agree with the subject.”

यह कपड़ा जयपुर में बनाया जाता है।This cloth is made in Jaipur.
हिन्दी कई प्रदेशों में बोली जाती है।Hindi is spoken in many states.

So the shape is always: perfective participle (the “-ed” part — बोला spoken, लिखा written, बनाया made) followed by a form of जाना “to go”, doing the work English’s “to be” does. And — this is the hook for our two verses — both words bend to match the subject’s gender and number.

लगायाplanted (participle) + गया हैजाना (perfect) → लगाया गया है “has been planted”

2 The two passives of Psalm 1, side by side

The psalm hands you the cleanest possible pair. Same recipe; the subject differs in gender (and the tense of जाना differs), so both parts shift — exactly as the handbook says they must:

Verse · subjectparticiple+ जाना= meaning
v3 · वृक्ष “tree” (masc.)लगायागया हैhas been planted
v4 · भूसी “chaff” (fem.)उड़ाईजाती हैis blown away

Look what agreement is doing. In v3 the subject is वृक्ष (masculine), so the participle is masculine लगाया and जाना shows as masculine गया. In v4 the subject is भूसी (feminine), so the participle turns feminine उड़ाई and जाना turns feminine जाती. The verb literally changes its dress to match who it is about — the same agreement instinct behind -ता ↔ -ती you already feel.

Why one says गया है and the other जाती है Both are जाना — just in different tenses, which the handbook notes the passive freely allows (“The passive can be used with any tense”, §14.1, p.178). v3’s गया है is the perfect (“has been …”): the tree stands already planted. v4’s जाती है is the habitual present (“is … / gets …”): chaff is forever being scattered. Don’t drill the tenses today — just notice the engine, जाना, is the same.

3 The agent: से = “by”

A passive often hides the doer. But v4 names it — and it is the very word you flagged last lesson: पवन से, “by the wind”. That little से you already know as “from / with” is also how Hindi says “by” when it points to the doer of a passive verb. Again, the handbook is precise:

Teach Yourself Hindi · §14.1 · printed p.179

“A passive verb tends to focus attention on the action done rather than the ‘doer’ or ‘agent’ of the action, who is often not mentioned at all. When mentioned, the agent takes the postposition से or, in a more formal style, के द्वारा.”

ताज महल शाहजहाँ के द्वारा बनाया गया था।The Taj Mahal was built by Shah Jahan.

So जो पवन से उड़ाई जाती है parses cleanly: जो “which” (the chaff, L3) · पवन से “by the wind” (agent) · उड़ाई जाती है “is blown away” (passive, feminine to match the chaff). “…which is blown away by the wind.”

4 Read the parked phrases — at last

लगाया गया हैhas been planted / is planted. From लगाना “to plant, set, fix” → perfective participle लगाया (masc. sg.) + गया है (जाना in the perfect). Agrees with masculine वृक्ष. (Wiktionary: लगाना.)
उड़ाई जाती हैis blown away. From उड़ाना “to blow away, cause to fly, winnow” → perfective participle उड़ाई (fem. sg.) + जाती है (जाना, habitual present). Agrees with feminine भूसी. (Wiktionary: उड़ाना.)
पवन सेby the wind — the agent, marked by से “by” (handbook p.179). The same से you met as “from / with”.
Two passives, two destinies Hear the contrast the grammar carries. The righteous man is लगाया गया — planted, done to him by Another, rooted and permanent. The wicked are उड़ाई जाती — blown, also done to them, but weightless, scattered पवन से, by the wind. Both are passive — neither is self-made — yet one is planted to stay and one is blown to vanish. The Hindi verb preaches the psalm.

6 🎉 Your win — vv.3–4, nothing parked

भजन संहिता 1:3–4 — fully parsed
…जो बहती नालियों के किनारे लगाया गया है।…which is planted by streams of running water.
…जो पवन से उड़ाई जाती है।…which is blown away by the wind.

You can now read every word of Psalm 1:1–4 with no crutch and nothing deferred — the passive was the last gate. Whenever you meet a participle (a “-ed”-type word) followed by जाना, read it as a passive: “is / was / has been …-ed”. And when a से sits beside it, that is the doer — “by”.
