Lesson 3 · The Relative Pronoun जो

The man who…

जो “the one who”, the वह … जो frame, and क्या ही धन्य है — closing Psalm 1:1 completely.
Vehicle: भजन संहिता 1:1 One skill: जो … वह relative clauses ~12 min

Recap — L1: चलता/होता/बैठता are habitual verbs. L2: a noun before a postposition goes oblique (plural → –ओं), and का/के/की means “of”. Today the last piece: the word that hangs that whole description onto one man.

Psalm 1:1 is really one sentence: “How blessed is the man — who doesn’t walk… who doesn’t stand… who doesn’t sit.” The hinge is जो, “the one who”. It is the single most useful word in the Psalter — “blessed is the man who…”, “those who…” appear on almost every page.

भजन संहिता · Psalm 1:1 · BSI OV
क्या ही धन्य है वह पुरुष
जो दुष्टों की युक्ति पर नहीं चलता,
और न पापियों के मार्ग में खड़ा होता;
और न ठट्ठा करनेवालों की मण्डली में बैठता है!
How blessed is the man who does not walk in the counsel of the wicked, nor stand in the path of sinners, nor sit in the assembly of mockers!

1 Relatives begin with ज-

Teach Yourself Hindi · §13.1 · printed p.162

“Hindi has several ‘relative-correlative’ constructions — structures for building sentences such as ‘When the train comes, I’ll go’ or ‘The person who wrote this is a fool’, in which two adjacent statements are related to one another as paired clauses.”

“Relative pronouns and adverbs all begin with a ‘ज-’, whereas interrogatives such as कब ‘when?’ begin with a ‘क-’.”

That single rule unlocks a whole family. Whenever a word starts with ज-, it is pointing back to something — “the one that”, “the place where”, “the time when”:

Interrogative (क-)Relative (ज-)
कौन who?जो the one who / which
कब when?जब when (then…)
कहाँ where?जहाँ where (there…)

2 जो — “the one who / which”

Teach Yourself Hindi · §13.4 · printed p.168

“जो means ‘(the one) who/which’. It is both singular and plural.”

जो आदमी बाहर खड़ा हैthe man who is standing outside
जो बोलता हैhe who speaks

“Such relative clauses are paired with correlative clauses, on the pattern of जब… तब (§13.1); the correlative here is वह / वे.”

Look how close the handbook’s example is to our verse Handbook: जो बोलता है — “he who speaks”.
Psalm 1:1: जो … नहीं चलता — “[the man] who does not walk”.
Same skeleton: जो + habitual verb. You already know the verb half from Lesson 1.

3 The वह … जो frame (it’s reversed from English)

In Psalm 1:1 the order is वह पुरुष (“that man”) first, then जो (“who”). The correlative वह “that (one)” names the person; जो opens the clause that describes him. English fuses them into one “who”; Hindi keeps the balanced pair:

वह पुरुष … जो … नहीं चलता
that man … who … does not walk  →  “the man who does not walk”

4 क्या ही धन्य है — the blessing frame

The verse opens with क्या ही. On its own क्या is the question word “what?” (a क- word — see §1). But क्या ही …! is an exclamation: “how very…!”. So:

क्या ही“how very…!” — an exclamatory intensifier (not a question here).
धन्यblessed, fortunate — the adjective (your glossary word from L1).
है“is” — the verb होना. How very blessed IS…
वह पुरुष“that man” — the one the whole जो-clause describes.

क्या as interrogative “what”: Teach Yourself Hindi, Unit 2 vocabulary, p.37. Its exclamatory use (क्या ही “how…!”) is the standard reading of this verse in the BSI OV.
