Lesson 8 · The future tense · stem + -एँगे

They will not stand

One easy ending, and verse 5 falls open. रह सकेंगे “will be able to stand”, ठहरेंगे “will abide” — how Hindi says will in a single word. One verse from the whole psalm.
Vehicle: भजन संहिता 1:5 One skill: the future tense ~10 min

Recap — you own all of vv.1–4, nothing parked: habitual verbs singular and plural (-ता है ↔ -ते हैं, L1/L6), oblique + postpositions and का के की (L2), the relative जो (L3), करता रहता है “keeps on doing” (L4), के समान “like” (L5), and the passive लगाया गया है / उड़ाई जाती है (L7). Every verb so far has been present. Verse 5 makes its first move into the future.

Until now the psalm has spoken in timeless present: the man walks, the tree is planted, the chaff is blown. Verse 5 turns to what will happen on the day of reckoning — and to say “will”, Hindi does something refreshingly simple. It adds one ending to the verb. No extra word.

भजन संहिता · Psalm 1:5 · BSI OV
इस कारण दुष्ट लोग अदालत में स्थिर न रह सकेंगे, और न पापी धर्मियों की मण्डली में ठहरेंगे।
Therefore the wicked will not stand in the judgment, nor sinners in the assembly of the righteous.

1 The future tense: one word, no auxiliary

Remember how the present habitual needed a helper tagged on — चलता है, होते हैं? The future drops the helper entirely. The whole tense lives in a single word. The handbook could not be more reassuring:

Teach Yourself Hindi · §9.1 “The future tense” · printed p.113

“The future tense is one of the easiest. It consists of a single word, with no auxiliary (unlike the English!).”

मैं बोलूँगा।I will speak.
शर्मा जी तुमको फ़ोन करेंगे।Sharma ji will phone you.
पंडित जी गाना गाएँगे।Pandit ji will sing a song.

You take the verb stem (the part left after dropping -ना, L-grammar) and add a future ending. The endings change for person, gender and number — but there is one tidy pattern, and the handbook spells it out:

Teach Yourself Hindi · §9.1 · printed p.113

“The masculine endings are just -ऊँगा for मैं, -एगा for all the other singulars; -ओगे for तुम, and -एँगे for all the other plurals. Feminines follow suit, with -ई as the final vowel.”

Subjectबोलना “to speak” → future (m.)meaning
मैं IबोलूँगाI will speak
यह / वह he, she, itबोलेगाhe will speak
तुम youबोलोगेyou will speak
वे / ये they (and हम, आप)बोलेंगेthey will speak

The highlighted row is the one verse 5 needs. Its two subjects — दुष्ट लोग “wicked people” and पापी “sinners” — are both masculine plural. So both their verbs take the very same ending: -एँगे.

2 The verse’s two futures share one ending

Watch -एँगे click onto each verb stem. Same rule, twice:

ठहरstem of ठहरना “to abide” + -एँगेplural future → ठहरेंगे “(they) will abide”
Subject (masc. pl.)stem+ future= in the verse
दुष्ट लोग wicked peopleरह (+ सक)-एँगेरह सकेंगे “will be able to stand”
पापी sinnersठहर-एँगेठहरेंगे “will abide”

The little न before each verb is the literary “not / nor” you already know (glossary). So the line says the wicked will not be able to stand, and sinners will not abide — the future of judgement, in two matching verbs.

3 रह सकेंगे — “will be able to”

One small extra. The first verb is not just रहेंगे “will stay” but रह सकेंगे “will be able to stay/stand”. That helper सकना means “to be able / can”, and it sits after a bare stem: रह सकना = “to be able to remain”. Crucially, when the phrase goes future, it is सकना that takes the ending — exactly the -एँगे from §9.1:

रहstem “stay/stand” + सकसकना “be able” + -एँगे → रह सकेंगे “will be able to stand”
Don’t over-think सकना today The skill for this lesson is the future ending — and you can see it does all the work in both सकेंगे and ठहरेंगे. Just file रह सकना away as “to be able to stay”; whenever a bare stem is followed by सकना, read it as “can / be able to (do that verb)”.

4 Read verse 5, word by word

इस कारणfor this reason, therefore. इस = oblique of यह “this” (before the noun’s force), + कारण “reason, cause” (m.). The hinge looking back to vv.1–4.
अदालत मेंin the judgment / court. अदालत “court of law, judgment” (f.) + में “in” — the postposition you know. (Wiktionary: अदालत.)
स्थिरfirm, steady, stable (adjective). स्थिर रहना/होना = “to stand firm, hold one’s ground”. (Wiktionary: स्थिर.)
रह सकेंगेwill be able to stand/remain. रहना “to stay, remain” (you know it from रहता है) → stem रह + सकना “be able” → future सकेंगे (masc. pl., for दुष्ट लोग).
पापीsinner(s); one who sins (from पाप “sin”). Here a masculine-plural subject; the word does not change shape. (Wiktionary: पापी.)
धर्मियों की मण्डलीthe assembly of the righteous. धर्मी “righteous one” → oblique plural धर्मियों (the -ओं/इयों you know, L2) + की “of” (feminine, agreeing with मण्डली) + मण्डली “assembly, congregation” (f.).
ठहरेंगे(they) will abide / stand / have a place. ठहरना “to stop, stay on, stand firm” (handbook p.115) → stem ठहर + future -एँगे (masc. pl., for पापी).
A future the grammar can carry Hear what the single-word future does here. The psalm has spent four verses in the timeless present — what the righteous and the wicked simply are. Now, with two quiet -एँगे endings, it lifts its eyes to the day of judgement: the wicked रह न सकेंगे — will not be able to stand; sinners न ठहरेंगे — will not abide. The chaff of v4 was already weightless; the future tense now tells us where the wind finally carries it. Grammar becomes prophecy.

You now read vv.1–5 with no crutch. Whenever you meet a verb ending in -एगा / -एँगे / -ऊँगा (feminine -एगी / -एँगी), read it as the future: “will …”. One ending, no helper word. One verse — 1:6 — stands between you and the whole psalm.
