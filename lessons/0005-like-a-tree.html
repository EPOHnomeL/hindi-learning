Lesson 5 · के समान & the oblique

Like a tree

के समान “like / similar to” — a compound postposition, and how it turns वह into उस. Into Psalm 1:3.
Vehicle: भजन संहिता 1:3 One skill: के समान “like” ~12 min

Recap — you own all of v1–v2: habitual verbs (L1), oblique + postpositions / की (L2), the relative जो (L3), and करता रहता है “keeps on doing” (L4). Verse 2 told us the blessed man delights in the law and meditates day and night. Verse 3 now paints him a picture.

What is such a man like? Verse 3 answers with one of the most beloved similes in the Psalms — he is like a tree planted by streams of water. The hinge word is के समान, “like / similar to”. That little phrase — and the way it bends वह into उस — is today’s one thing.

भजन संहिता · Psalm 1:3 · BSI OV
वह उस वृक्ष के समान है, जो बहती नालियों के किनारे लगाया गया है।
और अपनी ऋतु में फलता है, और जिसके पत्ते कभी मुरझाते नहीं।
इसलिये जो कुछ वह पुरूष करे वह सफल होता है॥
He is like a tree planted by streams of water, which yields its fruit in its season; and whose leaf never withers. Therefore whatever that man does prospers.

1 समान — “like, equal, similar”

On its own, समान (samān) is an adjective meaning “like, equal, similar, alike”. It is indeclinable — it never changes form for gender or number. (Wiktionary: समान.)

Glue the possessive के in front of it and you get the phrase के समान — “like / similar to”, “in the manner of”. It sits after the thing being compared to, the way all Hindi relationship-words do:

वृक्षtree + के समानlike → वृक्ष के समान “like a tree”

2 A compound postposition — and it takes the oblique

Why two words, के + समान? Because के समान is a compound postposition. The handbook defines the whole family precisely:

Teach Yourself Hindi · §6.4 · printed p.80

“§6.4  Compound postpositions.”

“These postpositions consist of two or more words, the first of which is the possessive के (or की if used with a feminine noun…). Like the one-word ‘simple postpositions’ (3.1), they take the oblique case.”

मेज़ के नीचेunder the table
घर के पासnear the house

This is the same law you learned in Lesson 2: a postposition forces the word before it into the oblique case. So the demonstrative वह “that / he” cannot stand as-is before के समान — it must become its oblique form, उस (you met this exact shift in Lesson 4: उसकी व्यवस्था):

वहthat (direct) → उसthat (oblique) + वृक्ष के समान → उस वृक्ष के समान “like that tree”

Notice the noun वृक्ष “tree” does not change shape — masculine nouns that don’t end in -ा look the same in the oblique singular. The oblique shows itself on the little pointing word वह → उस. Put it together with the copula है “is”, and you can read the whole opening clause:

वह उस वृक्ष के समान है “He is like that tree.”

3 The rest of verse 3

The simile unfolds in a relative clause (your friend जो from Lesson 3). You don’t need to master every word today — just see how the picture is built:

जो … लगाया गया हैwhich is planted — जो “which” (L3) + a new verb shape, लगाया गया है “has been planted”. That is the passive, a whole skill of its own — parked for a future lesson. For now, just read it as “(which) is planted”.
बहती नालियों के किनारेby streams of running water. बहती “flowing”, नालियों “channels/streams” (note the -ओं oblique-plural signal from L2!), किनारे “at the bank/edge of”.
अपनी ऋतु में फलता हैbears fruit in its season. ऋतु “season”; फलता है “bears fruit” — a habitual verb (L1) once more.
जिसके पत्ते कभी मुरझाते नहींwhose leaves never wither. पत्ते “leaves”; मुरझाते नहीं “do not wither” (habitual + नहीं, L1).
वह सफल होता हैhe/it prospers — literally “becomes successful”. सफल “successful, fruitful”. (Handbook glossary, unit 14.)
के समान — why it matters Scripture reasons constantly in similes — “like a tree”, “like the chaff”, “as a deer pants…”. के समान is one of the workhorses that carries them in the Hindi OV. Look at the very next verse, Psalm 1:4 — the wicked are उस भूसी के समान, “like that chaff”. Same construction, opposite picture. Learn it once, read it everywhere.

5 🎉 Your win — Psalm 1:3’s opening, and the simile

भजन संहिता 1:3 — the picture
वह उस वृक्ष के समान है,He is like that tree —
जो बहती नालियों के किनारे लगाया गया है।which is planted by streams of running water;
…जो कुछ वह पुरूष करे वह सफल होता है॥…whatever that man does, prospers.

You can now read the heart of verse 3: the blessed man of v1–v2 is उस वृक्ष के समान — like that tree by the water. And you can already spot the same shape waiting in v4 (उस भूसी के समान, “like that chaff”). One little compound postposition, two whole verses unlocked.
