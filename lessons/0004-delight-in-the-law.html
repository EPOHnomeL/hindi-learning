Lesson 4 · रहना & the Continuative

Day and night

रहना “to remain / keep on”, and करता रहता है “keeps on doing” — moving into Psalm 1:2.
Vehicle: भजन संहिता 1:2 One skill: करता रहना continuative ~12 min

Recap — you own all of v1: habitual verbs (L1), oblique + postpositions / की (L2), and the relative जो (L3). Verse 1 said what the blessed man does not do. Verse 2 turns to what he does — and does continually.

Verse 2 hangs on one verb you haven’t formally met: रहना, “to stay / remain”. It does double duty here — once meaning “remains (glad)”, and once as the engine of a new construction, करता रहता है, “keeps on doing”. That construction is everywhere in the Psalms (“I will continually praise…”), so it’s today’s one thing.

भजन संहिता · Psalm 1:2 · BSI OV
परन्तु वह तो यहोवा की व्यवस्था से प्रसन्न रहता;
और उसकी व्यवस्था पर रात दिन ध्यान करता रहता है।
But his delight is in the law of the LORD; and on his law he meditates day and night.

1 रहना — “to stay, to remain”

First as an ordinary verb. रहना means “to stay / remain / dwell”. In its habitual form (stem रह + ता, just like Lesson 1) it is रहता है “stays / remains”. In the verse it joins the adjective प्रसन्न “pleased, glad”:

प्रसन्नglad + रहताremains → प्रसन्न रहता “remains glad / delights”

(Note the missing है after रहता; — the same biblical auxiliary-drop you met in Lesson 1. It returns once, at the very end of the verse.)

2 करता रहता है — “keeps on doing”

Now the new construction. Put a habitual participle (the –ता form from Lesson 1) in front of रहना, and it stops meaning “remain” literally — it means the action keeps going:

Teach Yourself Hindi · §15.1 · printed p.191

“§15.1  करता रहना / जाना ‘to go on doing’.”

“An imperfective participle + रहना means ‘to keep on doing’: वह काम करती रही ‘she went on working’. The participle agrees with the subject.”

हम लोग रात भर काम करते रहे।We went on working all night.
अगर दिन भर पढ़ते रहोगे…If you go on reading all day…

Apply it straight to the verse. ध्यान करना means “to meditate / ponder” (literally “to do attention”). Stack the construction on top:

ध्यान करताmeditating + रहता हैkeeps on → ध्यान करता रहता है “keeps on meditating”

Not “meditates once” — meditates and goes on meditating, रात दिन “day and night”. The grammar itself carries the unceasing devotion of the verse.

3 The rest of verse 2

परन्तुbut — the contrast hinge. Verse 1: the don’ts. Verse 2: the opposite.
वह तो“he, on the other hand…” — तो is a little emphasis particle: “as for him”.
यहोवाthe LORD — Yahweh. A proper name; it never changes form. (See note below.)
व्यवस्थाlaw, instruction (the Torah). Feminine — which is why it takes की: यहोवा की व्यवस्था “the law of the LORD”.
सेpostposition “with / in” (Lesson 2). प्रसन्न … से = “glad in / pleased with”.
उसकीhis — उस (oblique of वह “he”) + की. The same की “of”, now on a pronoun: उस-की व्यवस्था “his law”.
रात दिनday and night (literally “night day”) — continually.
यहोवा — why it matters The BSI OV renders the divine name (יהוה, Yahweh) as यहोवा, where many English Bibles print “the LORD” in small caps. Wherever you see यहोवा in the Psalms, that is God’s covenant name. Worth recognising on sight — it appears in nearly every Psalm.

5 🎉 Your win — Psalm 1:2, and the v1→v2 turn

भजन संहिता 1:2 — fully parsed
परन्तु वह तो यहोवा की व्यवस्था से प्रसन्न रहता;But as for him — he delights in the law of the LORD;
और उसकी व्यवस्था पर रात दिन ध्यान करता रहता है।and on his law he keeps on meditating, day and night.

Hear the hinge: verse 1 was three things he does not do (नहीं … न … न); verse 2 is परन्तु — but — two things he does, and does without ceasing. You can now read both verses straight through.
