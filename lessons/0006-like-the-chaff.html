Lesson 6 · The plural habitual · –ते

Like the chaff

होता है → होते हैं — how the habitual verb you know changes shape for a plural subject. Into Psalm 1:4.
Vehicle: भजन संहिता 1:4 One skill: plural habitual होते हैं ~10 min

Recap — you own all of v1–v3: habitual verbs (L1), oblique + postpositions / की (L2), the relative जो (L3), करता रहता है “keeps on doing” (L4), and के समान “like / similar to” (L5). Every verb so far has had one man as its subject — so it was always singular: चलता है, होता है, सफल होता है.

Verse 4 swings the camera away from the one blessed man to the wicked — and they come as a crowd: दुष्ट लोग, “wicked people”. The moment the subject turns plural, the habitual verb you already know changes its ending: होता है becomes होते हैं. That single shift — singular → plural — is today’s one thing.

भजन संहिता · Psalm 1:4 · BSI OV
दुष्ट लोग ऐसे नहीं होते, वे उस भूसी के समान होते हैं,
जो पवन से उड़ाई जाती है।
The wicked are not so; they are like the chaff which the wind blows away.

1 The habitual agrees with its subject

Back in Lesson 1 you met the habitual: a verb stem + -ता, plus है — वह चलता है “he walks”. What you didn’t need yet is that the -ता ending changes to match the subject, exactly the way an adjective like अच्छा does. The handbook lays out the full set:

Teach Yourself Hindi · §6.1 “The imperfective present tense” · printed p.75

“The imperfective tense describes regular events or habits: ‘I speak Hindi’, ‘She lives in Delhi’, ‘We don’t smoke’. The participle — बोलता ‘speak’ … — is followed by हूँ / हो / है / हैं.”

वह बोलता हैhe speaks (m. sg.)
वे बोलते हैंthey speak (m. pl.)

So for a masculine subject the participle is -ता in the singular and -ते in the plural — and the little auxiliary moves from है (one) to हैं (many):

Singular — one man (v1–v3)
वह चलता हैhe walks
वह सफल होता हैhe prospers
Plural — the wicked (v4)
वे चलते हैंthey walk
वे … होते हैंthey are

2 Apply it to the verse: होते हैं

The verb in question is होना “to be / to become” (Lesson 1’s glossary). Its habitual singular is होता है “he is / becomes”. Make the subject plural — वे “they”, the wicked — and it becomes:

होता हैhe is (sg.) → होते हैंthey are (pl.)

So वे उस भूसी के समान होते हैं = “they are like that chaff”. Notice के समान (your Lesson 5 word) doing its job again — and the verb now plural to match वे.

3 The missing हैं — the negative drop returns

Look at the first clause: दुष्ट लोग ऐसे नहीं होते — “the wicked are not so”. The participle is plural (होते, matching दुष्ट लोग), but the हैं has vanished. That is not a misprint — it is a rule you first met in Lesson 1, and the handbook states it on the very same page:

Teach Yourself Hindi · §6.1 · printed p.75

“In the negative, the auxiliary verb (हूँ / हो / है / हैं) can be dropped.”

शर्मा जी गोश्त नहीं खाते।Sharma ji does not eat meat.

So नहीं होते stands for नहीं होते (हैं) — “are not”. The wicked are simply not like that blessed man of verses 1–3.

4 The rest of verse 4

दुष्ट लोगwicked people. दुष्ट “wicked” you met in Lesson 2 (दुष्टों की युक्ति “the counsel of the wicked”); लोग “people” makes it a plain plural subject.
ऐसेso / such / of this sort — from ऐसा, which inflects like अच्छा: masculine plural ऐसे. ऐसे नहीं होते = “are not so”. (Wiktionary: ऐसा.)
वेthey — the plural of वह “he/that”. (Its oblique form is उन, just as वह → उस.)
उस भूसी के समानlike that chaff — exactly the L5 shape (उस + noun + के समान). भूसी “chaff, husk” is feminine. (भूसी = chaff/husk.)
जो पवन से उड़ाई जाती हैwhich is blown away by the wind. पवन “wind” (masc); से here = “by” (the agent); उड़ाई जाती है “is blown away” — the passive again, the same shape as लगाया गया है in v3. Parked for its own future lesson; for now just read it as “(which) the wind blows away”.
Two pictures, one grammar Hear what the number is doing. The blessed man stood alone and rooted — one tree, singular: वह … सफल होता है. The wicked are a faceless, weightless crowd — plural, वे … होते हैं — and like भूसी, chaff, the wind scatters them. The grammar itself draws the contrast: the one who stays, and the many who blow away.

5 🎉 Your win — Psalm 1:4, and the singular→plural turn

भजन संहिता 1:4 — fully parsed
दुष्ट लोग ऐसे नहीं होते,The wicked are not so —
वे उस भूसी के समान होते हैं,they are like that chaff,
जो पवन से उड़ाई जाती है।which the wind blows away.

You can now read all four opening verses of Psalm 1 — and you’ve cracked the verb’s second face. Whenever the subject is plural, listen for the shift: -ता है → -ते हैं. The blessed man (singular) prospers; the wicked (plural) blow away. Same habitual tense, the number doing the work.
