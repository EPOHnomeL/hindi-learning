Lesson 1 · The Habitual Verb

Blessed is the man

Reading the backbone of Psalm 1:1 — and meeting the verb form that drives it.
Vehicle: भजन संहिता 1:1 One skill: the –ता habitual verb ~10 min

You already read the script. So we start where you actually are: meaning. Psalm 1 opens with a single long sentence describing the blessed man by what he does not do. Three verbs carry that sentence — and they are all the same grammatical shape. Learn that one shape today, and the skeleton of the verse opens up.

भजन संहिता · Psalm 1:1 · BSI OV
क्या ही धन्य है वह पुरुष
जो दुष्टों की युक्ति पर नहीं चलता,
और न पापियों के मार्ग में खड़ा होता;
और न ठट्ठा करनेवालों की मण्डली में बैठता है!
How blessed is the man who does not walk in the counsel of the wicked, nor stand in the path of sinners, nor sit in the assembly of mockers!

The three highlighted words — चलता, होता, बैठता — are habitual verbs. That is today's one thing.

1 What a habitual verb is

The present habitual describes a routine, a settled character, or a timeless truth: he walks, she lives in Delhi, we do not smoke. It is exactly the right tense for describing what kind of person someone is — which is what Psalm 1 is doing.

It is built in two pieces. Take the verb, drop the infinitive ending –ना to get the stem, then add –ता:

चलना – ना → चल + ता → चलता “walks”

Normally a form of होना (“to be”) follows it as an auxiliary: वह चलता है — “he walks.” The ending agrees with the subject in gender and number:

SubjectHabitualMeaning
masc. singularचलता हैhe walks
fem. singularचलती हैshe walks
masc. / mixed pluralचलते हैंthey walk

Grammar: Snell & Weightman, Teach Yourself Hindi, “The imperfective present tense”, printed pp. 74–77 (Handbook.pdf).

Why isn’t there a है after चलता and होता in the verse? In elevated and biblical Hindi the auxiliary है is often dropped — especially across a list, where it is stated only once at the end. So …नहीं चलता, …खड़ा होता; …बैठता है is one habitual pattern carried by the single है at the very end. Read all three as habitual, even though only the last shows its auxiliary.

2 The three verbs of Psalm 1:1

चलताfrom चलना — to walk, to go along. Here: “walks” (the man does not walk in the counsel of the wicked).
खड़ा होताfrom होना — to be / become; with खड़ा “standing”, it means “stands” (lit. “becomes standing”).
बैठताfrom बैठना — to sit. Here: “sits”.

Notice the negators: नहीं (“not”) and its short literary twin न (“and not / nor”) sit before the verb. The whole verse is one big “the man who does NOT walk… NOR stand… NOR sit…”.

4 Your win

You can now read the engine of Psalm 1:1. Strip the verse to its frame and you get a sentence you fully understand:

…वह पुरुष … नहीं चलता … न … खड़ा होता … न … बैठता है।
…that man … does not walk … nor stand … nor sit.

Parked for Lesson 2

The phrases between the verbs are next: दुष्टों की युक्ति पर (“in the counsel of the wicked”), पापियों के मार्ग में (“in the path of sinners”), …वालों की मण्डली में (“in the assembly of mockers”). These all use the oblique case + postpositions (की, के, में, पर) — the single most useful next step. We’ll take it whole next time.
