Lesson 2 · Postpositions & the Oblique Case

In the counsel of the wicked

The connective tissue of Psalm 1:1 — why दुष्ट becomes दुष्टों, and how की / के / में / पर work.
Vehicle: भजन संहिता 1:1 One skill: oblique case + postpositions ~12 min

Recap of Lesson 1: चलता / होता / बैठता are habitual verbs (stem + –ता), and biblical Hindi drops the है until the end of a list. Today: the phrases between those verbs.

Last time you read the verbs of Psalm 1:1. But the man walks, stands and sits somewhere — “in the counsel of the wicked”, “in the path of sinners”. Those phrases are built from postpositions, and they force the nouns to change shape. That shape-change is the single most useful pattern in Hindi. Learn it today.

भजन संहिता · Psalm 1:1 · BSI OV
क्या ही धन्य है वह पुरुष
जो दुष्टों की युक्ति पर नहीं चलता,
और न पापियों के मार्ग में खड़ा होता;
और न ठट्ठा करनेवालों की मण्डली में बैठता है!
How blessed is the man who does not walk in the counsel of the wicked, nor stand in the path of sinners, nor sit in the assembly of mockers!

1 Postpositions: English “in” comes after in Hindi

Teach Yourself Hindi · §3.1 · printed p.40

“In English, words like ‘in’ and ‘from’ are called prepositions because they precede a noun or pronoun (‘in the house’); in Hindi they follow a noun or pronoun, so they’re called postpositions (मकान में makān mẽ ‘in the house’). There are five ‘simple’ or single-word postpositions:”

PostpositionMeaningExample (handbook)
मेंinघर में — in the house
परon, uponमेज़ पर — on the table
कोtoराम को — to Ram
सेby, with, fromबस से — by bus
तकup to, as far asभारत तक — as far as India

Two of these are in our verse: पर (“upon”, rendered “in”) and में (“in”).

2 The oblique case: nouns change before a postposition

Teach Yourself Hindi · §3.2 · printed pp.41–42

“Hindi has two main cases. … all nouns were in the direct case, which is obligatory for verb subjects. But when a noun is followed by a postposition, it must transform into a different case, known as the oblique case.”

“Masculine ‘type 1’ nouns like कमरा kamrā ‘room’ change their final -ā to -e in the oblique singular (कमरे kamre):”

कमरे में सामान है। — “There is furniture in the room.”

“Nouns of all classes change in the oblique plural, which ends in -ओं (कमरों में kamrõ mẽ ‘in the rooms’).”

That last line is the key to our verse. Watch what happens to “wicked” when a postposition lands behind it:

दुष्ट (wicked — direct) + की (postposition) → दुष्टों की “of the wicked (ones)”

The -ओं ending is a flashing signal: “I am a plural noun, and a postposition is right behind me.” Once you see it, you know to look for the postposition that triggered it.

The three –ओं words in Psalm 1:1 दुष्टों (the wicked), पापियों (sinners), करनेवालों (doers / “those who do”) — all oblique plurals, each pulled into that shape by the की / के sitting behind it.

3 की / के — the postposition meaning “of”

The little word linking the two nouns is का (“of / ’s”). Like every postposition it forces the noun before it into the oblique — and it also changes its own ending to agree with the noun after it:

FormAgrees withIn the verse
काmasculine singular (direct)—
केmasculine, oblique / pluralपापियों के मार्ग में — in the path…
कीfeminineदुष्टों की युक्ति — …counsel of…

So की appears because युक्ति (counsel) and मण्डली (assembly) are feminine; के appears because मार्ग (path) is masculine and itself sits oblique before में.

Genitive का/के/की behaves as a postposition (Teach Yourself Hindi, §3.1–3.2); its agreement with the following noun is developed further in the adjective sections, §5.3 (pp.44–45).

4 Full parse — your win

Put it together and a phrase that was a wall is now transparent:

पापियों¹ के² मार्ग³ में⁴
“in the path of sinners”
पापियों ¹पापी “sinner” → oblique plural (–ओं), because a postposition follows.
के ²“of” — the masculine-oblique form of का, agreeing with मार्ग.
मार्ग ³“path / way” — masculine noun, sitting oblique before में.
में ⁴“in” — the simple postposition that triggered the whole oblique chain.
