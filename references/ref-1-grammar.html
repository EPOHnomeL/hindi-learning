Reference · Living cheat-sheet

Current grammar

The grammar Jonathan can use correctly so far. Terms follow Snell & Weightman's Teach Yourself Hindi; examples are anchored in the BSI OV Bible. This sheet is revised in place as understanding deepens.

Present habitual (imperfective present)

A verb form describing a routine, a general truth, or a repeated/timeless action — “walks”, “lives”, “does not smoke”. Built from the verb stem + a participle that agrees with the subject in gender and number, normally followed by the auxiliary है / हैं: -ता है (masc. sg., “he walks”), -ते हैं (masc. pl., “they walk”), -ती है / -ती हैं (feminine). Example: चलना “to walk” → वह चलता है “he walks”, वे चलते हैं “they walk”. In the negative, the auxiliary may be dropped: दुष्ट लोग ऐसे नहीं होते “the wicked are not so”. (Handbook §6.1, p.75.)

Avoid: “present tense”, “present simple” — too vague; Hindi has a separate present continuous. And don’t leave the participle as a fixed -ता: it must match a plural or feminine subject.

Verb stem

What is left of a verb after removing the infinitive ending -ना. चलना → चल, बैठना → बैठ, होना → हो. All tense endings attach to the stem.

Avoid: “root” — reserve that for later morphology discussion.

Postposition

A relationship word (in, on, to, from, of) that in Hindi comes after the noun, not before it — मकान में “in the house”. The five simple ones: में (in), पर (on), को (to), से (by/with/from), तक (up to). (Handbook §3.1, p.40.)

Avoid: “preposition” — that is the English order; Hindi reverses it.

Direct case / Oblique case

A noun has two cases. The direct case is its plain form, used for the subject of a verb. The oblique is the form it must take when a postposition follows it. Masculine -ा nouns shift -ा→-े in the oblique singular (कमरा→कमरे); nouns of all classes take -ओं in the oblique plural (कमरों, दुष्टों, पापियों). (Handbook §3.2, pp.41–42.)

Avoid: calling everything “the plural” — -ओं signals plural and oblique.

का / के / की — the genitive “of”

The postposition meaning “of / ’s”. Like all postpositions it puts the preceding noun in the oblique; it also agrees with the following noun — का (masc. sg.), के (masc. oblique/plural), की (feminine). दुष्टों की युक्ति “the counsel of the wicked”.

Avoid: treating का/के/की as three different words — they are one word agreeing.

Relative pronoun (जो)

The word “(the one) who / which”, used to attach a describing clause to a noun. It is both singular and plural. All Hindi relatives begin with ज- (जो who, जब when, जहाँ where), whereas the matching question words begin with क- (कौन, कब, कहाँ). (Handbook §13.1 p.162, §13.4 p.168.)

Avoid: confusing जो (relative “who”) with कौन (interrogative “who?”).

Correlative (वह … जो)

The “answering” word that pairs with a relative. Hindi keeps a balanced pair of clauses where English fuses them: वह पुरुष जो … = “the man who …” (lit. “that man, who …”). The relative clause (ज-) is matched by a correlative (वह/वे, तब, वहाँ). (Handbook §13.1, p.162.)

Avoid: expecting one fused word like English “who”; look for the ज-/वह pair.

Compound postposition (के समान, के पास, …)

A relationship-phrase of two or more words, the first of which is the possessive के (or की with a feminine word). Like the simple postpositions, it takes the oblique case on the noun before it — so वह “that” becomes उस in उस वृक्ष के समान “like that tree”. Common ones: के समान (like / similar to), के पास (near), के नीचे (under), के बाद (after), के लिए (for). (Handbook §6.4, p.80.)

Avoid: treating के समान as one indivisible word — it is के + समान, and the के is exactly the genitive you already know.

Passive (बोला जाना “to be spoken”)

Turns the verb around so the thing acted upon becomes the subject (“is planted”, “is blown”). Built from the perfective participle (the “-ed” part — बोला spoken, लिखा written, बनाया made, लगाया planted) + the auxiliary verb जाना “to go”. Both parts agree with the subject in gender and number, and it works in any tense: लगाया गया है “has been planted” (masc., with वृक्ष), उड़ाई जाती है “is blown away” (fem., with भूसी). The agent (doer), when named, takes से “by” (or formally के द्वारा): पवन से उड़ाई जाती है “is blown away by the wind”. (Handbook §14.1, pp.177–179.)

Avoid: reading गया / जाता / जाती here as the real verb “go” — with a participle in front, जाना is only the passive auxiliary (“to be”). And don’t confuse participle + जाना (passive) with stem + जाना (a compound verb, e.g. बोल जाना “to speak out”).

Future tense (बोलेगा “will speak”)

What will happen. Unlike the present, it needs no auxiliary — the handbook calls it “one of the easiest… a single word” (§9.1). An ending is added straight to the verb stem, agreeing with the subject in person, gender and number. Masculine endings: -ऊँगा (for मैं), -एगा (all other singulars), -ओगे (for तुम), -एँगे (all other plurals — वह, हम, आप, वे); feminines follow suit with -ई as the final vowel (-एगी, -एँगी). So ठहरना → ठहरेंगे “(they) will abide”, सकना → सकेंगे “(they) will be able” (Psalm 1:5, both plural). Three common verbs are irregular: होना → होगा, लेना → लेगा, देना → देगा. (Handbook §9.1, p.113.)

Avoid: reaching for a helper word like English “will / shall” — there isn’t one; the ending is the future. And don’t leave it singular for a plural subject: पापी (plural) takes ठहरेंगे, not ठहरेगा.

Potential (रह सकना “to be able to stay”)

“to be able to / can”. Formed from a bare verb stem + सकना (रह + सकना = “to be able to remain”). The सकना half carries the tense and agreement, while the first stem stays bare — so the future is रह सकेंगे “will be able to stand” (Psalm 1:5).

Avoid: inflecting the first verb (रहेंगे सकना ✗) — only सकना takes the ending; the lead verb stays as a stem.

Auxiliary (here, the है-auxiliary)

The “to be” verb (हूँ / है / हैं / हो) tagged onto a participle to anchor it in present time. In elevated/literary and biblical Hindi it is often dropped, especially in lists and timeless statements — which is why Psalm 1:1 reads चलता … होता … बैठता है (auxiliary only on the last verb).

Avoid: “helper verb” — ambiguous; Hindi also has true compound-verb helpers.
