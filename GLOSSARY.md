# Hindi Glossary

The canonical language for this workspace. Grammar terms follow Snell &
Weightman's *Teach Yourself Hindi*; vocabulary is anchored in the BSI OV Bible.
A term is added only once Jonathan can use it correctly.

## Grammar terms

**Present habitual** (imperfective present):
A verb form describing a routine, a general truth, or a repeated/timeless action
— "walks", "lives", "does not smoke". Built from the verb stem + **-ता / -ती /
-ते**, normally followed by the auxiliary है / हैं. Example: चलना "to walk" →
वह चलता है "he walks". (Handbook pp. 74–77.)
_Avoid_: present tense, present simple (too vague — Hindi has a separate present
continuous).

**Verb stem**:
What is left of a verb after removing the infinitive ending **-ना**. चलना → चल,
बैठना → बैठ, होना → हो. All tense endings attach to the stem.
_Avoid_: root (reserve for later morphology discussion).

**Postposition**:
A relationship word (in, on, to, from, of) that in Hindi comes **after** the noun,
not before it — मकान में "in the house". The five simple ones: में (in), पर (on),
को (to), से (by/with/from), तक (up to). (Handbook §3.1, p.40.)
_Avoid_: preposition (that is the English order; Hindi reverses it).

**Direct case** / **Oblique case**:
A noun has two cases. The **direct** case is its plain form, used for the subject
of a verb. The **oblique** is the form it must take **when a postposition follows
it**. Masculine -ा nouns shift -ा→-े in the oblique singular (कमरा→कमरे); nouns
of **all** classes take **-ओं** in the oblique plural (कमरों, दुष्टों, पापियों).
(Handbook §3.2, pp.41–42.)
_Avoid_: just calling everything "the plural" — -ओं signals plural **and** oblique.

**का / के / की** (the genitive "of"):
The postposition meaning "of / ’s". Like all postpositions it puts the preceding
noun in the oblique; it also agrees with the **following** noun — का (masc. sg.),
के (masc. oblique/plural), की (feminine). दुष्टों **की** युक्ति "the counsel **of**
the wicked".
_Avoid_: treating का/के/की as three different words — they are one word agreeing.

**Relative pronoun (जो)**:
The word "(the one) who / which", used to attach a describing clause to a noun.
It is both singular and plural. All Hindi relatives begin with **ज-** (जो who,
जब when, जहाँ where), whereas the matching question words begin with **क-** (कौन,
कब, कहाँ). (Handbook §13.1 p.162, §13.4 p.168.)
_Avoid_: confusing जो (relative "who") with कौन (interrogative "who?").

**Correlative (वह … जो)**:
The "answering" word that pairs with a relative. Hindi keeps a **balanced pair**
of clauses where English fuses them: वह पुरुष **जो** … = "the man **who** …" (lit.
"that man, who …"). The relative clause (ज-) is matched by a correlative (वह/वे,
तब, वहाँ). (Handbook §13.1, p.162.)
_Avoid_: expecting one fused word like English "who"; look for the ज-/वह pair.

**Auxiliary** (here, the है-auxiliary):
The "to be" verb (हूँ / है / हैं / हो) tagged onto a participle to anchor it in
present time. In elevated/literary and biblical Hindi it is often **dropped**,
especially in lists and timeless statements — which is why Psalm 1:1 reads
चलता … होता … बैठता है (auxiliary only on the last verb).
_Avoid_: helper verb (ambiguous — Hindi also has true compound-verb helpers).

**Continuative (करता रहना)**:
A habitual participle (–ता form) + **रहना** "to remain", meaning **"to keep on /
go on doing"** — the action continues. The participle agrees with the subject:
वह काम करती रही "she went on working"; ध्यान करता रहता है "he keeps on meditating".
(Handbook §15.1, p.191.)
_Avoid_: reading रहता literally as "remains" when a –ता participle sits in front of it.

**Conjunct verb (ध्यान करना)**:
A noun (or adjective) + a light verb — usually **करना** "to do" or **होना** "to be"
— fused into one verb. ध्यान करना "to meditate" (lit. "to do attention"); प्रेम करना
"to love"; विश्वास करना "to believe". The noun stays put; only करना/होना conjugates.
_Avoid_: translating the noun and करना separately ("do attention").

## Vocabulary (BSI OV, Psalm 1)

**यहोवा** (yahovā):
The LORD — the divine covenant name (Yahweh, יהוה). Indeclinable (never changes
form). The BSI OV uses it where English Bibles print "the LORD" in small caps.

**व्यवस्था** (vyavasthā):
Law, instruction, ordinance — the Torah / God's law. Feminine (so: यहोवा **की**
व्यवस्था "the law of the LORD").

**परन्तु** (parantu):
But — the contrast conjunction. Marks the turn from Psalm 1:1's "does not…" to
1:2's "but he does…". A more literary/formal twin of लेकिन.

**प्रसन्न** (prasanna):
Pleased, glad, delighted. प्रसन्न रहना "to remain glad / to delight (in)".


**धन्य** (dhanya):
Blessed, fortunate, happy. The opening word of Psalm 1's blessing —
क्या ही धन्य "how blessed!".

**पुरुष** (puruṣ):
Man, person. Masculine noun. (Printed in the OV as पुरूष.)

**जो** (jo):
Who / which — the relative pronoun that opens a relative clause ("the man **who**
walks…"). Pairs later with वह "that (one)".

**चलना** (calnā):
To walk, to move, to go along. Habitual: चलता है "walks".

**बैठना** (baiṭhnā):
To sit. Habitual: बैठता है "sits".

**होना** (honā):
To be / to become. The most important verb in Hindi; also the source of the
है-auxiliary. खड़ा होना "to stand" (lit. "to become standing").

**नहीं** (nahīṁ):
Not — the standard negator, placed before the verb. न (na) is its shorter,
more literary twin, used for "and not / nor".
