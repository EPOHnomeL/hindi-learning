# Plural habitual (–ते हैं) taught — Psalm 1:4 read

Lesson 6 (`0006-like-the-chaff.html`) taught the **number/gender agreement of the
imperfective (habitual) present**, grounded in handbook §6.1, printed p.75:
masculine singular participle `-ता है` → masculine plural `-ते हैं`
(`वह सफल होता है` → `वे … होते हैं`). The negative auxiliary-drop was re-surfaced
on the same page (`दुष्ट लोग ऐसे नहीं होते` = "the wicked are not so", with `हैं`
dropped) — it ties back to the L1 biblical drop, now with a textbook rule behind it.

Vehicle was **Psalm 1:4** (BSI OV): `दुष्ट लोग ऐसे नहीं होते, वे उस भूसी के समान
होते हैं, जो पवन से उड़ाई जाती है।` The verse also reused **के समान** (L5) and
**दुष्ट** (L2) as deliberate spaced recall, which NOTES flagged as wanted.

**Can now be assumed:** Jonathan can inflect a known habitual verb for a plural
masculine subject (`-ता है ↔ -ते हैं`) and recognise it on sight, and explain the
negative auxiliary-drop. He has read Psalm 1:1–1:4 end-to-end. New vocab added to
the glossary: दुष्ट, लोग, वे, ऐसा, भूसी, पवन.

**Still open / next ZPD candidates:**
- **Feminine habitual** (`-ती है / -ती हैं`) — the other half of the agreement set;
  not yet seen in a verse, so lower priority until a feminine subject appears.
- **The passive** (`लगाया गया है` in v3, `उड़ाई जाती है` in v4) — deferred twice now.
  It is the one parked grammar skill that blocks *full* mastery of v3 and v4, and
  is well-teased. Strong candidate, but a bigger jump (handbook Ch.14). `से` = "by"
  (the passive agent) is the natural hook, since it just appeared in `पवन से`.
- **Psalm 1:5–6** — the last two verses, to complete the mission's first waypoint
  (whole psalm). v5 brings the future/subjunctive (`रह सकेंगे`, `ठहरेंगे`), v6 the
  contrast `जानता है … नाश हो जाएगा`.

Comprehension on L4 and L5 was confirmed (5/5 on both). Treat singular+plural
habitual, oblique+postpositions, की, जो/correlative, करता रहता है, and के समान as
a solid working floor.
