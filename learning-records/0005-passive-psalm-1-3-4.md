# The passive (participle + जाना) taught — Psalm 1:3–4 fully unparked

Lesson 7 (`0007-planted-and-blown-away.html`) taught the **Hindi passive**, the one
grammar skill that had been parked (teased in L5 and twice in L6) and that the
learner explicitly asked for ("Lets finish the last thing to complete Verses 3
and 4 the passive").

Grounded in handbook **§14.1, printed pp.177–179** (verified by rendering the
scanned spread): the passive = **perfective participle + the auxiliary जाना**, and
**"both parts agree with the subject"** (quoted near word-for-word per NOTES). Also
quoted the agent rule (p.179): the doer, when named, takes **से "by"** (or formally
के द्वारा).

Vehicle was the matched pair the psalm itself supplies:
- **v3** `लगाया गया है` "has been planted" — masculine, agreeing with `वृक्ष` (tree);
  जाना in the **perfect**.
- **v4** `उड़ाई जाती है` "is blown away" — feminine, agreeing with `भूसी` (chaff);
  जाना in the **habitual present**. Plus `पवन से` "by the wind" as the agent — the
  से="by" hook left dangling in L6.

The masc/fem contrast was the teaching engine: it makes "both parts agree" visible
(लगाया/गया ↔ उड़ाई/जाती) and gives the learner an incidental first taste of feminine
participle agreement without making it the lesson. Verse text re-verified
character-for-character against bible.com 819 (HHBD).

**Can now be assumed:** Jonathan can recognise a passive on sight (participle +
जाना), read it as "is/was/has been …-ed", explain that both parts agree with the
subject, and read से as the agent "by". **Psalm 1:1–4 is now fully parsed with
nothing deferred** — every word readable unaided.

New glossary entries: लगाना, उड़ाना, जाना (incl. its passive-auxiliary role). New
grammar-reference term: "Passive (बोला जाना)" with the §14.1 recipe, agreement, and
agent rule.

**Still open / next ZPD candidates:**
- **Psalm 1:5–6** — the final two verses, to complete the mission's first waypoint
  (the whole psalm). v5 brings future/subjunctive (`रह सकेंगे`, `ठहरेंगे`), v6 the
  closing contrast (`यहोवा … जानता है … दुष्टों का मार्ग नाश हो जाएगा`). This is now
  the strongest candidate — the only psalm-text left, and the mission's stated goal.
- **Passive tense drill** — `बनाया जाता है / बनाया गया / बनाया जाएगा` across present,
  past, future. Useful consolidation, but secondary to finishing the psalm.
- **Feminine habitual** (`-ती है / -ती हैं`) in an *active* verb — still not seen as a
  plain active subject in a verse; the learner has now met feminine agreement only
  inside the passive (उड़ाई जाती). Teach when a feminine active subject appears.

L6 comprehension was 4/5: the one "miss" (fill-0) was the learner typing
`वे चलते हैं` where the exact-match answer was `चलते हैं` — i.e. they *added* the
correct pronoun, so it was right in substance, a string-match artefact, not a real
error. Treat singular+plural habitual, oblique+postpositions, का/के/की, जो, करता
रहता है, के समान, and now the passive as a solid working floor.
