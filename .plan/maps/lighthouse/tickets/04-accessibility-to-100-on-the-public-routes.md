# Accessibility to 100 on the four public routes

## Question

Lighthouse's Accessibility category is the cheapest 100 on this map and the one most
likely to be genuinely worth having: this is a reading product, and some of its learners
will be using a screen reader or a keyboard because that is how they read.

Nothing here is measured yet, so **01's failed-audit list is the scope, not this
ticket's suspicions.** What the tree suggests is worth looking at, read on 2026-09-21:

- **Accessible names on icon-only controls.** `aria-label` appears at all in 17 of the
  component files and the counts are thin and uneven (1 in `Landing.tsx`, 1 in
  `NavItem.tsx`, 2 in `ui.tsx`, 2 in `Dashboard.tsx`). Several of those files are on the
  public path: `Landing`, `LandingSections`, `PhoneMocks`, `InterestForm`, `SignIn`,
  `LocalePicker`, `DonateSection`, `SiteFooter`, `Brand`, `Logo`.
- **Two `<div onClick>` and five `role="button"` sites.** A div with a click handler is
  not focusable and not announced; a `role="button"` needs a key handler and a
  `tabIndex` to be honest. Check whether any of the seven is on a public route.
- **Contrast.** The palette is per-tenant, generated by `buildTenantThemeCss` from
  `src/design/tokens.ts` and baked into a pre-paint `<style>`. So contrast is not one
  fixed pair of colours: `text-soft` on `bg-paper` may pass on the default skin and fail
  on a tenant's. The `hover:text-accent` nav links in the legal layout and the
  `text-soft` footer text are the obvious first candidates.
- **Tap-target size.** `AppTabs` is a bottom tab bar, and the reader chrome is full of
  fixed-height controls; `technical-foundation/09`'s notes record `min-h-[38px]`
  line-clamped card subtitles, which is under the 48 px Lighthouse wants.
- **The lesson iframe is a separate document and is not on a public route here.**
  `ArtifactView` notes the lesson body carries its own `<h1>`. Out of scope: no public
  URL in this map's four renders a lesson.

An audit score of 100 is necessary and not sufficient, which is why this ticket asks for
a human. axe catches a missing accessible name; it does not catch a control whose name
is present and wrong, or a focus order that is technically valid and useless. The
sign-in flow embedded in the Landing is the one to walk, because it is the conversion.

Contrast fixes touch `src/design/tokens.ts`, which is shared with every tenant skin and
with the certificate stage. A token change is not a local change: check what else reads
the token before moving it, and prefer a fix at the call site if only one call site is
failing.

## Done when
- The Accessibility category scores 100 on all four public URLs with the 01 harness, and
  the Answer quotes the before scores from 01's baseline.
- Every icon-only control on a public route has an accessible name, and every clickable
  non-button is either a real button or focusable with a key handler.
- The whole landing page including the embedded sign-in flow is completed using only a
  keyboard, and the Answer says so as a walked claim, naming what focus order was
  observed and anything that was fixed as a result.
- If `src/design/tokens.ts` was changed, the Answer names every surface that reads the
  changed token and states that each was looked at, the certificate stage included.
- The Answer states whether the default skin was the only one checked, or which tenant
  skins were, since contrast is per-tenant.
