R1 on the desktop, three takes

Same R1 shell, three different desktop structures. The first spreads nothing out. The second turns the tabs into a sidebar. The third keeps one working column and adds a summary rail. Each frame has its own state, and every flow works in all of them.

D1One calm column

The phone layout, given room and nothing else: the same two-row header stretched across the top, the content in one centered column. Managing a course is a settings page, not a dashboard, so width buys margins, not more columns.

D2Sidebar peers

The tab row becomes a left rail: Sharing, Users, Settings as icon rows, always visible. The content keeps one column, headed by the course name and the edition button. The classic desktop management pattern, and the biggest departure from the phone.

D3Column plus summary rail

The phone header and one working column, plus what the desktop uniquely has room for: a quiet right rail stating what is true right now, pinned while you scroll. The controls stay in one predictable line; the rail is read, never operated.

What each refinement trades

All three keep the approved tab row. The differences are where the edition picker lives and what the owner sees first, judged at 360px with the seller and 20-edition stress cases on.
