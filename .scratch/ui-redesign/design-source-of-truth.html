Screens & layout

One entry per screen the app renders. Each shows its route, its purpose, a labelled layout wireframe of the regions, what fills each region, and the states it must handle. Overlays (dialogs) are listed as their own entries — they float over whatever screen opened them. This is the reference the component library in Part B is built to satisfy.

/

The signed-in learner's home. A header, then the courses they own as a card grid, then everything shared with them, then everything they've purchased. The header's gear is the single entry point to account preferences (it replaced the old separate globe + theme toggle).

overlay · gear in S1 header

The gear in the dashboard header. Bundles the three preferences a learner tweaks into one place: display name (their account name — also what prints on the certificate), app language, and theme. This replaced the header's separate globe picker + theme toggle. Guests don't get it — they have no account — so guest surfaces keep the standalone C-locale picker instead.

S8

Certificate

/certificate/[token]

The standalone, shareable proof of completion — an A4-landscape document carrying the learner's name, the course title, and the subject emblem struck into a gold medallion. On screen it's a premium artefact (foil sheen, pointer tilt); it prints and exports to PDF as a flat engraved document. The palette is frozen to the default gold/green even under a tenant skin.

S9

Shared & buyer reader (read-only)

/share/[token] · unlocked course

What someone a course is shared with — or who bought an edition — sees: a read-only learner. Same nav as the owner minus Course settings and the language switcher: they read the edition they were given/bought and can't change it. They track their own progress, ask the owner questions, and earn their own certificate. A buyer is identical to a shared viewer — only how they got in differs.

Group 3

Settings dialogs — account & course

Two distinct dialogs. Account settings (the header gear, S3) holds the learner's own preferences — display name, app language, theme. Course settings (S4) holds one course's details, emblem and lifecycle. Keeping them separate stops "my preferences" and "this course" from bleeding together.

Account = who you are, across the app. Course = this one course.

Group 4

Share & editions panel

Source English plus each translation, each with live status. Sharing is invite-by-email + a real on/off toggle for the public link (lock → globe), the URL revealed only when it's on.

Each language is a tab; + adds one. English's link is on — open the हिन्दी tab and flip its toggle. العربية is translating, Español failed.

Group 5

Course sidebar cleanup

Once S3/S4 absorb the owner controls, the two full-width buttons crowding the lesson nav collapse into a single Course settings entry; the edition switcher & theme stay pinned at the bottom.
